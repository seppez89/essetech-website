What a fractional IT manager does, and what they don't

We're based in Adelaide and work with businesses across Australia remotely. If you're in Adelaide and want your manager in the office, see our fractional IT manager in Adelaide page.

A fractional IT manager does the work an in-house IT manager would do, for part of the month. That means planning your IT and your IT budget, managing your vendors (including your IT provider, internet and phone carriers, software suppliers and printer leases), overseeing your security starting with Microsoft 365, and reporting to the owners or the board in plain English.

A fractional IT manager doesn't answer your helpdesk phone. They don't replace your managed service provider either. Most of our clients keep their provider for day-to-day support, and we make sure that provider delivers what you pay for. If you have no provider, we also offer managed IT and IT support, and we'll tell you plainly whether you need one, the other or both.

A typical month

Each month depends on what's happening in your business. The regular pieces look like this.

Planning

We keep a 12 to 24 month IT plan covering hardware replacement, software, security work, AI adoption and budget. It's tied to what your business is trying to do, so every spending decision has a reason behind it.

Vendor calls

We deal with your vendors for you. We review contracts and renewals, check that support tickets are handled properly and push back on quotes that don't make sense.

Security review

We review your Microsoft 365 setup, multi-factor authentication, admin accounts, backups and device management against the Essential Eight. You get a list of gaps ranked by risk. We fix them or get your provider to fix them. Each month we check that what was fixed stays fixed.

Reporting

You get a written report in plain English covering security status, open risks, project progress and IT spend.

Who it suits

This service is built for businesses with roughly 10 to 80 staff and no IT manager. Usually someone has ended up owning IT on top of their real job: the office manager, the finance lead or one of the owners.

A stable business with 10 to 20 staff usually needs fewer days a month. A business with 50 or more staff, or one in the middle of a migration or security project, usually needs more. We agree a starting number of days in the first meeting and review it after three months.

It's a good fit if:

  • You pay an IT provider, or several vendors, and aren't sure what you're getting for the money.
  • A client, insurer or auditor has asked about your cyber security and you couldn't answer with confidence.
  • A project is coming up, such as an office move, a Microsoft 365 migration or an AI rollout, and nobody owns it.
  • Your board or owners want regular, plain reporting on IT risk and spend.

Fractional IT manager or managed service provider

A managed service provider runs your helpdesk, monitors devices and fixes things when they break, usually for a monthly fee per user. A fractional IT manager works for you and makes the calls an in-house manager would make.

Managed service provider Fractional IT manager
Main job Support, monitoring and fixes Planning, oversight and decisions
Works for Its own contract with you Your business
Typical pricing Per user per month Set days per month
Reports to your board Rarely Yes
