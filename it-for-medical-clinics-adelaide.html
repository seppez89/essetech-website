When the practice manager is also the IT person

Clinic IT is lots of small pieces: reception PCs, consult room computers, the practice software, Microsoft 365 email, printers and scanners, the internet and the phones. Patient information runs through most of it.

When something goes wrong it's usually the practice manager who rings the vendor, resets the password or tries to work out who still has access after a staff member leaves. That's a lot to carry on top of running a clinic, and it's hard for them to know whether things are set up properly.

It's a good fit if:

  • Your practice manager is the go-to for IT problems on top of their real job.
  • You're not sure who has admin access to email, Microsoft 365 or the clinic's other systems.
  • People have left the practice and nobody has checked their logins were switched off.
  • You pay an IT provider or several vendors and aren't sure what you're getting for the money.
  • You've got a new practice manager or new rooms coming and want the IT in order before then.

What I look after as your IT manager

Your practice software vendor looks after their product. I look after everything around it, so the practice manager can get back to the practice.

Devices

Reception and consult room PCs, laptops, printers and scanners. I keep a list of what you've got, set new machines up properly and plan replacements before one fails in the middle of a session.

Logins and access

I set up new staff, remove access people no longer need when they leave, and write down the admin accounts and who owns them. The practice manager stops being the only person who knows the passwords.

Microsoft 365 and email

I check multi-factor authentication, review who has admin rights and keep email and shared files in order. Essetech is a Microsoft CSP reseller, so I can supply and manage your Microsoft 365 licences as well.

Backups

I check what's being backed up and what isn't, including practice data if it sits on a server in your building. Then I test the backups by restoring from them.

Security

I run endpoint protection on your machines, filter email and patch out of hours, so updates don't interrupt consults.

Vendors

The practice software vendor, internet and phone providers, printer leases and any IT provider you already pay. When one of them needs something from your end, I deal with it.

AI help for the front desk and back office

A clinic has plenty of admin that follows the same pattern every day. I set AI up inside your own secured environment, your staff check what it drafts, and I start with admin that doesn't touch patient records. Staff also get a written AI use policy that says what can and can't go into it.

Calls at busy times

Calls that would ring out while reception is flat out can be answered by AI, which takes the caller's details so the front desk can call back.

The practice inbox

Sorting the shared clinic inbox so supplier emails, staff questions and general enquiries are filed by topic, with a short summary of what's new.

Practice admin

Drafting staff notices, policy updates and a plain summary of the monthly numbers for the principal.

The same questions, again

An assistant on your website can answer common questions from your own information, like opening hours, parking and the services you offer. It hands over to a person when it matters.

I start with one job that's clear and worth the effort, and build it. You see it working before you commit to more. If it earns its place, I build the next one.
