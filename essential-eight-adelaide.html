The eight controls in plain English

These are the eight strategies as listed by the Australian Signals Directorate in Essential Eight explained.

1. Patch applications

Keep programs such as web browsers, Microsoft Office and PDF readers up to date, so known holes are closed quickly.

2. Patch operating systems

Keep Windows and macOS updated, and replace versions the maker no longer supports.

3. Multi-factor authentication

A second step when signing in, such as a code or an app prompt, so a stolen password is not enough on its own.

4. Restrict administrative privileges

Only the people who need admin rights have them, and they don't use those accounts for email or web browsing.

5. Application control

Only approved programs can run, which stops most malicious software from starting.

6. Restrict Microsoft Office macros

Block macros that come from the internet and allow only the ones your business needs.

7. User application hardening

Turn off risky features in browsers and Office that attackers rely on, such as web ads and old plug-ins.

8. Regular backups

Back up important data, keep copies where an attacker can't reach them and test that you can restore.

The maturity levels explained

Each control is measured at one of four levels. You pick a target level, then bring all eight controls up to it.

LevelWhat it means
Maturity Level ZeroThere are gaps in your basic defences.
Maturity Level OneYou are protected against common attacks that use widely available tools and are aimed at anyone.
Maturity Level TwoYou are protected against attackers willing to put more time and effort into targeting you.
Maturity Level ThreeYou are protected against skilled attackers who adapt their methods to a specific target.

The Australian Signals Directorate says that, generally, Maturity Level One may be suitable for small to medium enterprises. See its Essential Eight maturity model FAQ.

Who asks about the Essential Eight

The Essential Eight is not a legal requirement for most private businesses. It is the benchmark Australian organisations get measured against, so the question tends to arrive from outside.

When it does, a written record of where you stand on each control is the quickest way to answer it.

You'll usually hear about it from:

  • A cyber insurer, on the application or renewal form.
  • A government or enterprise customer, in a tender or supplier questionnaire.
  • An auditor or your board, after a breach somewhere in your industry.
  • Your own staff, after a phishing email gets through.
